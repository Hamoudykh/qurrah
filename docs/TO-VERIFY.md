# Items to check with a trusted scholar or source

Everything below is marked **[TO VERIFY]**. Nothing in Qurrah is a fatwa. The Quran text
itself is not on this list because it is never typed into the app: it is loaded from
Quran.com (or Tanzil via AlQuran.cloud) and shown with surah and ayah numbers so you can
check it against a printed mushaf.

How to use this list: open each sunnah.com link (the app links every reference) and check
that the summary in the app matches the narration and that the grading is right.

## 3. Fiqh summaries (Pray → What to recite, and the khushu' notes)

Please check that each difference of opinion is stated fairly:

* Al-Fatihah: a pillar per the majority; **wajib** per Hanafis; the follower behind an imam who recites aloud is disputed.
* A surah after al-Fatihah in the first two rak'ahs: sunnah per the majority, wajib per Hanafis.
* Reversing mushaf order is valid; some consider it makruh.
* Du'a in sujud in a language other than Arabic during obligatory prayer is disputed; many allow it in voluntary prayers.
* Responding to ayahs (asking, seeking refuge) was in voluntary night prayer; doing it in obligatory prayer is disputed.
* Where to look in prayer: "many scholars recommend the place of prostration" (other views exist).
* App tip, not a ruling: practise a new surah in voluntary prayers before using it in obligatory ones.
* Planner defaults: the planner keeps mushaf order inside a prayer, prefers a longer first rak'ah, gives longer recitation to Fajr and shorter to Maghrib, and uses one surah in both rak'ahs only when a single surah is known.

## 4. Text written for this app

* **40 daily-verse reflections** (`js/data/daily.js`) in English, Arabic and Hebrew. Each one
  restates only the plain meaning of the verse's Saheeh International translation (checked
  against the API during development) and adds a small action. They are labelled
  "not tafsir". Please read all 40, especially the Arabic and Hebrew lines. They paraphrase
  on purpose and never quote Quranic wording.
* **Arabic and Hebrew interface text** (`js/i18n/ar.js`, `js/i18n/he.js`) and the Arabic and
  Hebrew versions of the virtues, khushu' tips and fiqh notes were written by the developer
  (an AI assistant), not by a professional translator. Have native speakers review them,
  especially the religious terminology in Hebrew transliteration.
* Hadith summaries in Arabic are sometimes close to the original wording. They are labelled
  as summaries ("خلاصات للمعنى"). A reviewer may prefer to replace them with the exact matn
  from a printed source.

## 5. Translations and sources

* **Hebrew Quran translation**: Dar Al-Salam Center (Quran.com translation 233). It is the
  only Hebrew translation Quran.com offers. Ask someone you trust whether it is acceptable.
* **Transliteration**: Quran.com's, offered only as an aid. Learn pronunciation from the audio and a teacher.
* **Quran.com API v4** (`api.quran.com/api/v4`): the public endpoint worked throughout development.
  The Quran Foundation also runs an authenticated API. If the public endpoint changes, the app falls back to AlQuran.cloud
  (Tanzil), but long-term availability and terms of use should be reviewed before wide distribution.

## 6. Prayer times, qibla, dates

* **Method suggestions by country** are made only for SA (Umm al-Qura), EG (Egyptian),
  PK (Karachi), US/CA (ISNA), GB/IE (Moonsighting Committee), AE (Dubai), KW, QA,
  SG/MY/ID/BN (Singapore), TR (Diyanet), IR (Tehran). Everywhere else, including Israel and
  Palestine, the default is **Muslim World League**. Compare with your local mosque's
  timetable and use the per-prayer minute adjustments.
* **Umm al-Qura and Ramadan**: Adhan's docs note a +30 min 'Isha adjustment during Ramadan
  for Umm al-Qura. The app does not apply this automatically; use the 'Isha adjustment.
* **High latitudes**: the default rule is Adhan's recommendation (Middle of the Night below
  48°, Seventh of the Night above). Local scholarly practice varies.
* **Hijri date**: from the browser's Umm al-Qura calendar. It can differ by a day from local moon sighting.
* **Qibla**: great-circle (shortest path) direction, the method in standard use today. Phone
  compasses can be off by several degrees; the app says so and shows a calibration tip.

## 7. Levels

The Easy/Medium/Hard assignment is a teaching choice based on word counts (see PLAN.md), not
a religious ruling. A Quran teacher may prefer a different order, for example teaching
At-Tariq before Ash-Shams. The order is in `js/data/catalog.js`.
